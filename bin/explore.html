How to use this
  1. Start from the result. Knobs open at what the autoresearch loop (bin/program.md) settled on; orange labels differ from the live config. Press "live config" to see today's board, "autoresearch tuned" to come back.
  2. Read the first three tiles. They are the loop's objective, scored on your own involvement over every work hour of 4 weeks: did the board show a thread you later stepped into before you got there (catch), and how much of the top 10 is the kind of thread you join at all (purity). Switch the label to "jev judge" for jev's opinion of what needed you.
  3. Pick the schedule you intend to keep. Default is every work hour so no formula can tune itself to three instants; click hours off to see what a sparser habit costs.
  4. Sweep: pick an objective and a knob to see the objective across its whole range, or sweep all to rank every knob by how much its best value helps. "Sweep all" on the involvement objective is the loop, in the browser.
  5. Hover anything in the sidebar for what the knob means, what the values do, and what the loop found when it moved it. Hover a title on the board for its full breakdown.
  6. One label, every number. The label selector above the tiles decides what "needed" means; the objective, the tier tiles, the probability fit, the rank bands, the misses and the row markers all derive from that one choice. Switch it and everything moves together.
  7. Tiering was not in the loop. The method radios decide how red and orange are derived from the scores; the tier tiles show what each costs and catches under the selected label.

    How this is computed

    Each thread is replayed from its reply timeline: at a checkpoint only the replies posted before it exist. The score is the dashboard's one-line formula, re-implemented here for exploration, plus the topic terms: channel weight ^ exponent × (base + activity + topic votes) × atrophy × alive-boost × damping × topic gates. Working hours are precomputed by the app's own business_hours_between.

    One label, every number. The label selector picks what "needed" means, and every number on the page is derived from that one predicate: the catch and purity tiles, the objective the sweep optimizes, the tier tiles, the probability fit behind the calibrated-probability method, the rank bands, the misses list and the row markers. Nothing is fitted on one label and reported on another. Involvement: a thread you stepped into (replied to, did not start) is needed at every look before your first reply, and "the kind of thread" for purity is any thread you ever joined. Judge: jev's p(needs Scott) ≥ 0.6, same at every look. Habit: needed at the look before you replied, plus late bloomers at their takeoff look.

    catch = per needed thread, the best credit over its needed looks, 1 inside the top 10 else 10/rank, averaged over threads. purity = per look, the share of the top 10 that the label marks, averaged over looks. The probability tiering fits P(marked | score) as a class-balanced logistic on log(1 + score) over every row of every scheduled board, refit whenever a knob or the label moves.
